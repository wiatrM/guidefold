# Motion-site archetypes

## Ambient loop with copy-safe field

Use when motion supplies atmosphere and the page carries the message. Require a stable focal subject, large low-detail copy region, muted loop, poster, crop map, and either matching end frames or a deliberate crossfade. References: 002, 005, 006.

## Action plate with typographic framing

Use when physical movement supplies urgency. Keep text outside the subject path, use a short resolved shot instead of pretending a hard-cut action clip loops cleanly, and give the poster the same readable composition. Reference: 007.

## Bottom-anchored product composition

Use when the video has strong light or texture above and useful ground near the bottom. Place the value statement and proof at the lower edge, define breakpoint-specific contrast, and limit glass panels to real utilities. Reference: 003.

## Editorial cutout with moving type

Use when identity, photography, or fashion direction matters more than product explanation. Separate background, type plane, and transparent subject. Keep the subject deterministic and let only the type or background move. Reference: 004.

## Layered still-scene scroll story

Use when geometry must remain exact and the story has discrete spatial chapters. Build transparent depth planes, drive transforms from normalized scroll state, define reversible enter/hold/exit segments, and keep panels in semantic order. Reference: 001.

## Scroll film

Use only when meaningful intermediate frames carry a transformation. Define a timestamped beat sheet, exact final state, reverse behavior, poster, frame budget, and Save-Data replacement. Do not convert an ambient loop into a scrubbed film.

## Combination rule

Choose one archetype for the page. A secondary mechanic may solve navigation or input feedback, but it cannot introduce another visual thesis. Glass, marquee, pointer drift, stagger, and parallax are mechanics, not archetypes.
